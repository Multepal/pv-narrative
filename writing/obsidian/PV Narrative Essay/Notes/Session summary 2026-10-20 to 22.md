# Session summary: methods and app revision (20–22 October 2026)

A long working session that reorganized the Streamlit app around the argument, made the choice of parameters principled, added an external test against editors' divisions, and fixed two pipeline bugs that affected every result. All tables are now generated by scripts from the app's own pipeline.

## 1. The argument and the app's structure

The sidebar now follows the argument (config `pages:`, built into sections by `app.py`):

| Section | Pages | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Model | Clustering (TF-IDF) | The method |
| 2 · Choose k | Choose k (ARI Grid Search) | k by cross-edition agreement; n_chunks by stability |
| 3 · Test | Cross-Edition Comparison, Robustness (Cosine-Sim) | Held-out translations; method robustness |
| 4 · Paradigmatic Axis | Relations (PCA / LSA), Content (NMF Topics), Temas (Thematic Check) | Structure: oppositions, content, external check |
| 5 · Syntagmatic Axis | Boundary Agreement, Episode Boundaries, Editors' Divisions, Narrative Play, Narrative Transitions, Narrative DCT | Event: segmentation (checked against editors), then sequence |
| 6 · Prediction | Chapter Topics, Christenson Text Reader | The model applied to chapters it never saw |
| Resources | Annotation Coverage, Entity Index | Supporting data |

Framing decisions:

- **One partition, several lenses.** TF-IDF → Ward HAC is the primary partition. PCA/LSA reads the relations between classes (oppositions), NMF reads their content, Cosine-Sim (second-order similarity) is a robustness check. The other methods corroborate; they don't compete.
- **Structure vs. event.** ARI (agreement on classes) belongs to the paradigmatic axis and chooses k. Boundary F1 (agreement on breaks) belongs to the syntagmatic axis and measures divisions.
- **Three meanings of a boundary** (Editors' Divisions page): a change of paradigmatic class; a break in lexical continuity (resonance, Barron et al. 2018); an editor's division. The manuscript is undivided, so editorial divisions are readings, and the model is judged as one more reader among them.
- **Reliability vs. validity.** Cross-edition agreement shows the structure is stable; agreement with independent editors tests whether it is the narrative's division.

## 2. Choosing parameters

**k (Choose k page).**
- k\* is chosen by **excess over chance**: mean pairwise agreement minus the 95th percentile of a cyclic-shift baseline (one sequence rotated against the other, which keeps block structure and breaks alignment). This replaced the old rule of "ARI ≈ 0 = chance" and the ad hoc exclusion of k = 2.
- **Held-out design:** k is chosen on K'iche' ↔ K'iche' pairs only; K'iche' ↔ translation agreement is reported as an independent test.
- k is capped at n_chunks / 3 (near-singleton partitions agree trivially; this also fixed silent clamping when k ≥ n_chunks) and chosen among k ≥ 3 (k = 2 measures only the primary division).
- The page reports k\* with its robustness across the grid, not a "best" single cell. Excess isn't comparable across n_chunks, because the chance baseline falls with fewer chunks; the old "best n_chunks = 20" reflected a low baseline and noise.

**n_chunks (Choose k → Resolution).**
- Chosen by **stability**: token-level ARI between each edition's k = 6 partition at n and at n ± 10%. Stability rises until about 32 chunks, then plateaus (32–50). The rule takes the **plateau onset**, the coarsest stable resolution (Ben-Hur et al. 2002; Lange et al. 2004).
- The earlier 33 had been chosen by eye from the scree and dendrogram; the rule confirms it sat at the start of the plateau. **The reporting setting is now n_chunks = 32.**

## 3. Bugs found and fixed

- **Tokenizer.** Every app vectorizer used scikit-learn's default token pattern, which split K'iche' words at the glottal-stop apostrophe (k'ajolom → "ajolom") and dropped one-letter words, affecting about 40% of tokens in Christenson's and Colop's orthography. Now `model.token_pattern: '\S+'` and `utils.tfidf_vectorizer` / `count_vectorizer` factories are used by all 26 vectorizers. (The research notebooks had an override, `(?u)\b[a-z'][a-z']+\b`, which itself drops accented Spanish letters; `local_lib`'s parser splits on whitespace.)
- **Unseeded NMF.** The nndsvda initialization uses a randomized SVD; labels at k ≥ 9 varied between runs. Seeded via `model.random_state`.
- **Uncentered LSA** in the grid searches (TruncatedSVD, n = 10) didn't match the PCA/LSA page; now centered PCA with the page's n_components.
- **Stale or hard-coded results:** Chapter Topics projected an old notebook topic matrix; Narrative Play hard-coded its model divisions and its whole transition table; the author's divisions were copied into three pages. All now computed live or read from config.
- Smaller fixes: CLAUDE.md launch and requirements paths, the Episode Boundaries citation (Barron, Huang, Spang & DeDeo 2018, not "Jurafsky et al."), `use_container_width`, a pipeline-notebook typo.

## 4. New and rebuilt pages

- **Editors' Divisions** (new). Scores the k = 6 boundaries against seven editors' part divisions (Brasseur, Recinos, Edmonson, Tedlock, Colop, Bazzett, Sotelo; config `divisions`), the author's 8 divisions (reported separately: not independent of the models), and Christenson's chapters. Two detectors (class changes; resonance peaks with the same boundary budget), a chapter-resolution test (each chapter assigned out of sample to the nearest class centroid), an editorial-consensus table, and the model placed among the editors.
- **Cross-Edition Comparison.** Excess-over-chance charts by pair group (within K'iche', K'iche' ↔ translation), boundary F1 with a tolerance in whole chunks, an "Agreement at One Setting" table (n, mean, range, chance, excess, p).
- **Content (NMF Topics).** HAC scree beside the coherence chart; a Topics vs. HAC Clusters table with ARI against chance.
- **Clustering (TF-IDF).** Defaults from the reporting setting or a handoff from Choose k; the scree marks k\* and the elbow is read as a check on it.
- **Boundary Agreement.** Tolerance in whole chunks (the old τ = α/2(k−1) assumed k−1 boundaries and saturated); Ensemble mode reports k\*, robustness and held-out score by method instead of a recommended combination.
- **Chapter Topics, Narrative Play, Text Reader** now use the app's own model and the config's divisions.
- Shared modules: `grid_search_boundary_core` (pipeline and agreement), `grid_search_ui` (grid views), `divisions.py` (editorial scoring), `resolution.py` (stability).

## 5. Results (reporting setting: TF-IDF → Ward, k = 6, 32 chunks, min_df 5, max_df 0.5)

- **k\* = 6 by ARI for all four methods**, chosen on K'iche' pairs; held-out excess +0.27 to +0.33; k\* = 6 on all pairs for every method and metric (Table 2). Boundary F1 gives k = 3 for Cosine-Sim and NMF, but that peak is unstable across the grid and at or below chance at the reporting setting.
- **Cross-edition agreement** beats chance for every method, within K'iche' (ARI 0.86–0.88) and across translation (0.74–0.78), p ≤ 0.003; dropping Christenson's Ximénez changes no mean by more than 0.035 (Table 1).
- **Sequence:** A B C D C A E F in 7 of 8 editions under NMF (Recinos adds a B), and at nearly every resolution.
- **Editors' divisions:** class changes beat chance against 4 of 7 editors (Edmonson, Tedlock, Colop, Bazzett) at every resolution from 20 to 40 chunks. At chapter resolution, 4 of 7 at 32 chunks (4–6 across 32–40); exact at chapters 9, 41 and 65. Resonance corroborates only weakly (0 of 7 at 32). No class change marks chapter 57; the class structure supports the division at 65, as in Tedlock and Colop. The model agrees with the editors (F1 0.66) as well as they agree with each other (0.64) (Table 3).
- **Resolution:** the structural results are invariant across the stability plateau; the chapter-level prediction is the sensitive one and is reported as a band (Table S1).
